## Market Positioning and Growth Outlook

- Chipotle’s business model is centered on company‑owned and partner‑operated restaurants that generate revenue directly from food and beverage sales. — 10-K line 182: As of December 31, 2025, we owned 3,938 Chipotle restaurants throughout the United States (“U.S.”) and 104 international Chipotle restaurants… Our revenue is derived from sales by our restaurants.
- The company’s growth strategy, branded as the “Recipe for Growth,” focuses on accelerating restaurant expansion, modernizing technology, and sharpening competitiveness across five key areas. — 10-K line 184: Chipotle is a brand… Our Recipe for Growth strategy leans into what uniquely differentiates Chipotle, with a focus on accelerating growth and sharpening competitiveness.
- A core pillar of the strategy is to modernize the business model with industry‑leading technology, including artificial intelligence and a relaunched rewards program, to improve the guest and team experience. — 10-K line 187: •Modernize our business model with industry-leading technology, including leveraging AI and relaunching our Rewards Program, to elevate the experience for our guests and teams;
- Digital ordering and delivery are strategic growth drivers, accounting for more than a third of food and beverage revenue, with a focus on expanding the Chipotlane drive‑through format. — 10-K line 208: Our digital platform continues to be a strategic driver of our growth. Digital sales represented 36.7% of food and beverage revenue in 2025… we have made digital ordering convenient… by building more Chipotlanes, which is our drive through format for guest pick‑up of digital orders.
- The fast‑casual, quick‑service, and casual dining sectors are highly competitive, with rivals competing on taste, price, food quality, service, location, and digital engagement. — 10-K line 247: The fast‑casual, quick-service, and casual dining segments of the restaurant industry are highly competitive… Competition includes… taste, price, food quality and presentation, customer service, location, convenience, brand reputation…
- Chipotle’s emphasis on responsibly sourced ingredients and its “Responsibly Raised®” branding is intended to provide a differentiation advantage, though many competitors make similar quality claims. — 10-K line 272: We continue to believe our commitment to higher‑quality and responsibly sourced ingredients resonates with guests and gives us a competitive advantage; however, many of our competitors also make claims related to the quality of their ingredients…
- In the third quarter of 2026 the company opened eighty‑four new company‑owned restaurants, including fifty‑three Chipotlanes, and five partner‑operated locations, highlighting ongoing expansion momentum. — EX-99.1 line 26: Opened 84 company‑owned restaurants, with 53 locations including a Chipotlane. We also opened five international partner‑operated restaurants.
- Management projects low‑single‑digit comparable restaurant sales growth for the full year 2026 and expects to open three hundred fifty to three hundred seventy new restaurants, most featuring Chipotlanes. — EX-99.1 line 41: Full year comparable restaurant sales growth in the low single digit range… 350 to 370 new restaurant openings, which includes 10 to 15 international partner‑operated restaurants. Around 80% of new company‑owned restaurants will have a Chipotlane.
- Sustainability is embedded in the brand promise, with all meat sourced under “Responsibly Raised®” standards and produce sourced according to Food with Integrity guidelines. — 10-K line 191: In Chipotle‑owned restaurants, we strive to serve only animal products that are raised in accordance with criteria… We brand our meats as “Responsibly Raised®.” We also seek to use responsibly grown produce…
- The company mitigates commodity price volatility through forward contracts, diversified supplier bases, and direct procurement of key raw materials. — 10-K line 307: We try to mitigate future price risk through forward contracts, strong partnerships with key suppliers, directly managing key raw material procurement and diversifying our supply base and countries of origin; however, these activities may not fully insulate us from increases in costs…
- Chipotle is heavily dependent on information‑technology systems for point‑of‑sale, digital ordering, supply‑chain tracing, and other core processes, exposing the business to cybersecurity and system‑failure risks. — 10-K line 290: We are heavily dependent on information technology systems… Our ability to effectively manage our business… depends significantly on the consistent availability, reliability and security of these systems.
- The company invests in emerging technologies such as artificial intelligence and machine learning, though it acknowledges that these initiatives may not deliver expected efficiencies and could introduce new risks. — 10-K line 292: We continue to invest in and utilize emerging technologies… These new technologies may not deliver the expected efficiencies and could expose us to new risks…

## Investment Recommendation

**Buy** — Chipotle’s growth engine is anchored by a company‑owned restaurant network, a digital platform that now accounts for more than a third of its food and beverage revenue, and a clear expansion roadmap that emphasizes the Chipotlane drive‑through format. The firm’s Recipe for Growth strategy blends accelerated site rollout, technology modernization—including artificial intelligence and a refreshed rewards program—and a sustainability narrative that differentiates its brand. Financially, the company delivers consistent revenue expansion, a solid operating margin and an exceptionally high return on equity while maintaining a zero‑debt balance sheet, which together suggest ample capacity to fund further openings and shareholder returns. However, the business operates in a highly competitive fast‑casual landscape, faces volatile commodity and labor costs, and is heavily reliant on information‑technology systems that expose it to cyber and operational risks. Balancing these dynamics, the upside from continued digital adoption and store growth outweighs the identified headwinds, supporting an investment‑positive stance.
- Risk: Intensifying competition across taste, price, service and digital engagement could erode market share and pressure margins.
- Risk: Commodity price volatility and limited hedging effectiveness may compress profitability if ingredient costs rise faster than menu price adjustments.
- Risk: Labor cost inflation and regulatory changes around scheduling and wages could increase operating expenses.
- Risk: Reliance on complex IT infrastructure and third‑party providers creates exposure to cybersecurity breaches and system outages.
- Risk: Execution risk tied to rapid restaurant expansion, including site selection, construction delays and supply‑chain capacity constraints.
