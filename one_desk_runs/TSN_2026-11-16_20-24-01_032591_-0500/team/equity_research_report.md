# TYSON FOODS, INC. (TSN)
Analysis date: 2026-11-16

## Overview

- Tyson is a world‑class food company with a broad portfolio of iconic brands and about 124,000 team members worldwide — 10-K line 130: Tyson Foods, Inc. and its subsidiaries (collectively, the “Company,” “we,” “us,” “our,” “Tyson”) ... had approximately 124,000 employees (“team members”) on October 3, 2026.
- The company operates a fully vertically integrated chicken production process that spans breeding, feed, hatchery, grow‑out, processing and distribution — 10-K line 132: We operate a fully vertically integrated chicken production process.
- Tyson also processes live cattle and hogs into dressed meat, case‑ready products and specialty items — 10-K line 133: We also process live fed cattle and hogs and fabricate dressed beef and pork carcasses into primal and sub‑primal meat cuts, case‑ready beef and pork and fully cooked meats.
- A dedicated venture subsidiary invests in innovative protein, animal‑welfare and sustainability technologies — 10-K line 135: Tyson New Ventures, LLC is used to broaden our exposure to innovative forms of protein and opportunities to improve animal welfare, water management, packaging and land stewardship...

## Market Positioning and Growth Outlook

- Tyson competes globally with other food producers and seeks market leadership through advertising, price, product safety, brand strength and innovation — 10-K line 171: Our food products compete with those of other food producers and processors and certain prepared food manufacturers.
- The principal competitive elements include advertising, consumer and trade promotions, price, product safety and quality, brand identification, innovation, breadth and depth of product offerings, availability of products, customer service and credit terms — 10-K line 175: The principal competitive elements are advertising, consumer and trade promotions, price, product safety and quality, brand identification, innovation, breadth and depth of product offerings, availability of products, customer service and credit terms.
- Walmart represents the largest single customer, contributing roughly nineteen percent of consolidated sales; no other customer exceeds ten percent — 10-K line 169: Walmart Inc. accounted for approximately 19.0% of our fiscal 2026 consolidated sales. ... No other single customer or customer group represented more than 10% of fiscal 2026 consolidated sales.
- Tyson markets products in roughly one hundred forty countries and regions, with major sales markets across North America, Europe, Asia and the Middle East — 10-K line 177: We sold products in approximately 140 countries and regions in fiscal 2026.
- Management expects domestic protein production to be flat to slightly higher in fiscal 2027, providing a neutral to modestly positive supply backdrop — EX-99.1 line 112: For fiscal 2027, the United States Department of Agriculture (USDA) indicates domestic protein production (beef, pork, chicken and turkey) will be flat to a slight increase compared to fiscal 2026 levels.
- Liquidity is projected to stay well above the company’s minimum target, supporting operations and strategic initiatives — EX-99.1 line 134: We expect total liquidity, which was $4.1 billion as of October 3, 2026, to remain well above our minimum liquidity target of $1.0 billion.
Insight: Tyson’s fully vertically integrated chicken model gives it control over breeding, feed, processing and distribution, a capability highlighted by its claim "We operate a fully vertically integrated chicken production process" — 10-K line 132.

## Valuation Insights

The 10‑K filing covers the full fiscal year that ended on October 3 2026, with comparable prior‑year data for the year ended September 27 2025. The earnings release presents twelve‑month results that align with the same fiscal period, noting that the fourth quarter contained an extra week which was adjusted out for non‑GAAP measures.
Revenue for the year was reported as modestly higher than the prior year, reflecting overall growth despite legal contingency adjustments that reduced the topline in both years.
Operating income for the year was essentially unchanged from the prior year, indicating flat profitability at the GAAP level.
GAAP earnings per share were slightly lower than the prior year, while adjusted earnings per share showed a more pronounced decline, highlighting the impact of the additional week and other non‑GAAP adjustments.
Operating margin, as disclosed in the release, was low, consistent with the thin profitability typical of a high‑volume, low‑margin food‑production business.
The company generated positive cash from operations and free cash flow, with free cash flow derived by subtracting capital expenditures from operating cash flow, as defined in the non‑GAAP reconciliation.
Total stockholders’ equity declined modestly year over year, providing a basis for return‑on‑equity calculations, though the resulting return was described as low.
Long‑term debt figures were not disclosed in the provided data set, so leverage ratios such as debt‑to‑equity or net‑debt‑to‑EBITDA cannot be computed or evaluated.
No market price for the common stock, price date, or price source was supplied; consequently, valuation multiples that rely on a price denominator (e.g., price‑to‑earnings) are unavailable, and any assessment of valuation limits must be qualified accordingly.
Peer data were not supplied, therefore comparative analysis against industry peers cannot be performed within the constraints of the available evidence.
The filing indicates a change in segment reporting: the International segment, previously non‑reportable, was elevated to a reportable segment beginning in the first quarter of fiscal 2026, which affects segment‑level profitability analysis.

## Future Developments and Strategic Recommendations

- Tyson conducts continuous research and development, including new product innovation, ingredient simplification, automation and breeding stock improvements — 10-K line 182: We conduct continuous research and development activities which include new product innovation, product improvements, ingredient simplification, manual process automation in our processing facilities and grow‑out operations and chicken breeding stock improvements.
- The company’s network optimization plan is a key catalyst, but its success depends on efficient implementation and may incur multi‑year costs — 10-K line 234: The success of our network optimization plan, or any other financial or operational optimization plans, will depend in part on our ability to successfully implement them in an efficient and effective manner.
- Disease outbreaks such as African swine fever or highly pathogenic avian influenza could materially affect livestock supply, product demand and brand perception — 10-K line 225: Supply of and demand for our products can be adversely impacted by disease outbreaks impacting animals, animal products and livestock, such as African swine fever (“ASF”), bovine spongiform encephalopathy, foot and mouth disease, highly pathogenic avian influenza (“HPAI”)...
- Fluctuations in commodity and feed‑grain prices can erode gross margins and limit the ability to pass costs to customers — 10-K line 219: Fluctuations in commodity prices and in the availability of raw materials, especially feed grains, live cattle, live swine and other inputs, could negatively impact our earnings.
- Intensifying competition and private‑label pressure may force price concessions or higher promotional spending, weighing on profitability — 10-K line 224: The food industry in general is intensely competitive. We face competition from other food producers and processors ... From time to time, in response to competitive pressures or to maintain market share, we may need to reduce the prices for some of our products...
- Climate‑change risks could raise input costs, disrupt production and require additional capital expenditures — 10-K line 291: Climate change may have a long‑term adverse impact on our business.
- Judgment: Maintain and expand R&D investment to sustain product innovation and mitigate competitive pressure — 10-K line 182: We conduct continuous research and development activities which include new product innovation, product improvements, ingredient simplification, manual process automation in our processing facilities and grow‑out operations and chicken breeding stock improvements.
- Judgment: Prioritize execution of the network optimization plan while monitoring cost overruns, as management warns that implementation may be more difficult, costly or time‑consuming than expected — 10-K line 234: The success of our network optimization plan, or any other financial or operational optimization plans, will depend in part on our ability to successfully implement them in an efficient and effective manner.

## Investment Recommendation

**Hold** — Tyson Foods leverages a fully vertically integrated chicken operation and a broad portfolio of iconic brands to sustain its market presence across domestic and international markets. The company generates positive cash from operations and maintains liquidity well above its minimum target, providing flexibility for strategic initiatives. However, operating profitability remains low and essentially flat year over year, reflecting thin margins typical of high‑volume food production and exposure to commodity price swings, disease risks, and competitive pricing pressures. Without clearer evidence of margin expansion or transformational growth from the network optimization plan, the balanced outlook supports a neutral stance.
