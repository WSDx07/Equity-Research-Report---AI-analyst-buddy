# Walmart Inc. (WMT)
Analysis date: 2026-11-19

## Overview

- Walmart describes itself as a people‑led, technology‑powered omnichannel retailer that helps customers save money and live better by offering both physical stores and eCommerce options. — 10-K line 175: Walmart Inc. ("Walmart," the "Company" or "we") is a people-led, technology-powered omnichannel retailer dedicated to helping people around the world save money and live better by providing the opportunity to shop in both retail stores and through eCommerce, and to access our other service offerings.
- The company’s strategy focuses on price leadership (EDLP/EDLC), digital expansion, and building trust with customers. — 10-K line 176: Our strategy is to make every day easier for busy families, operate with discipline, sharpen our culture and become more digital, and make trust a competitive advantage.
- Walmart U.S. operates 4,611 stores across all 50 states, Washington D.C., and Puerto Rico. — 10-K line 187: Walmart U.S. is our largest segment and operates 4,611 stores in the U.S., including in all 50 states, Washington D.C. and Puerto Rico.
- The U.S. segment provides a seamless omnichannel experience that links stores and eCommerce. — 10-K line 188: Omnichannel. Walmart U.S. provides a convenient and seamless omnichannel experience to customers, integrating retail stores and eCommerce.
- AI‑powered tools are being used to improve customer experiences, associate productivity and operational efficiency. — 10-K line 182: Our strategies increasingly include the use of AI-powered tools to support customer and member-facing experiences, associate productivity and operational efficiency across our ecosystem.
- The firm is continuously investing in supply‑chain automation and fulfillment capabilities. — 10-K line 233: We continue to invest in supply chain automation and our fulfillment and delivery capabilities to better serve our customers.

## Market Positioning and Growth Outlook

- Walmart competes with a broad set of brick‑and‑mortar, eCommerce and omnichannel players across many retail formats and service categories. — 10-K line 222: Competition. We compete with brick and mortar, eCommerce and omnichannel retailers operating discount, department, retail and wholesale grocery, drug, dollar, variety and specialty stores, supermarkets, supercenter-type stores, membership-only warehouse clubs, gasoline stations and social commerce platforms, as well as companies that offer services in digital advertising, fulfillment and delivery services, health and wellness and financial services.
- Key competitive levers include everyday low pricing, omnichannel services, expanding digital advertising and marketplace, new store openings/remodels, and technology/automation investments. — 10-K line 230: Investing in technology, automation, and our associates to deliver growth, expand operating margins and improve returns.
- In the most recent quarter, net sales grew 5.2%, and when adjusted for currency effects the growth was 4.5%. — EX-99.1 line 10: Revenue growth of 5.2%, up 4.5% in constant currency (cc)1
- Global eCommerce sales rose 17% year‑over‑year, driven by store‑fulfilled pickup and delivery. — EX-99.1 line 12: eCommerce sales up 17% globally
- U.S. eCommerce sales increased 23% in the quarter, showing strong integration of online and physical channels. — EX-99.1 line 62: eCommerce sales increased 23%, with strength in store-fulfilled delivery, advertising, and marketplace
- The global advertising business grew 25% and the U.S. advertising segment grew 37% in the quarter. — EX-99.1 line 23: Global advertising business2 up 25%.
- Membership fee revenue grew 17% globally, and a new bundled membership offering was launched in the U.S. — EX-99.1 line 24: Membership fee revenue grew 17% globally
- International operations contributed roughly one‑fifth of consolidated net sales, underscoring the importance of global growth. — 10-K line 373: During fiscal 2026, our Walmart International operations generated approximately 19% of our consolidated net sales.

## Future Developments and Strategic Recommendations

- Management identifies failure to execute the omnichannel strategy and the cost of eCommerce/technology investments as a strategic risk that could materially affect market position and financial performance. — 10-K line 283: Failure to successfully execute our omnichannel strategy and the cost of our investments in eCommerce and technology may materially adversely affect our market position, net sales and financial performance.
- Health pandemics, natural disasters, and other macro events are enumerated as operational risks that could disrupt operations and earnings. — 10-K line 306: Global or regional health pandemics or epidemics could negatively impact our business, financial position and results of operations.
- Robust information‑system continuity is highlighted as critical, with the company emphasizing the need to keep business‑critical systems running without interruption. — 10-K line 327: Given the number of individual transactions we have each year, it is crucial that we maintain uninterrupted operation of our business-critical information systems.
- Judgment: Continue prioritizing AI‑driven tools for customer experience and associate productivity, as the filing explicitly cites AI as a core element of the growth strategy (line 182) and the company warns that under‑investment could harm market position (line 283). — 10-K line 182: Our strategies increasingly include the use of AI-powered tools to support customer and member-facing experiences, associate productivity and operational efficiency across our ecosystem.
- Judgment: Accelerate supply‑chain automation projects to mitigate the operational risk described in line 233 and to sustain the eCommerce growth demonstrated in the earnings release (line 12). — 10-K line 233: We continue to invest in supply chain automation and our fulfillment and delivery capabilities to better serve our customers.

## Investment Recommendation

**Hold** — Walmart’s omnichannel model continues to capture strong eCommerce momentum and advertising growth, while disciplined cost control and a solid balance sheet generate ample cash flow. However, operating profitability is under pressure as investments in AI, supply‑chain automation and store remodels weigh on margins, and the company flags execution risk to its omnichannel strategy. The mix of resilient top‑line growth and margin compression suggests a neutral stance pending clearer evidence of margin recovery or superior returns on technology spend.
