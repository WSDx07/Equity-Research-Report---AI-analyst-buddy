# COSTCO WHOLESALE CORP /NEW (COST)
Analysis date: 2026-11-05

## Overview

- The company’s business model is a membership‑based warehouse club that combines low‑price, limited‑SKU merchandising with high volume and rapid turnover in both physical warehouses and e‑commerce sites. — 10-K line 137: We operate membership warehouses and e-commerce sites based on the concept that offering low prices on a limited selection of nationally‑branded and private‑label products in a wide range of categories will produce high sales volumes and rapid inventory turnover.
- The firm positions its value proposition around consistently lower prices for high‑quality goods, contrasting with broader‑assortment retailers. — 10-K line 144: Our strategy is to provide our members with a broad range of high‑quality merchandise at prices we believe are consistently lower than elsewhere.
- A deliberately narrow SKU count (under 4,000 per warehouse) is used to drive velocity and simplify inventory management. — 10-K line 144: We carry less than 4,000 active stock keeping units (SKUs) per warehouse in our core warehouse business, significantly less than other broadline retailers.
- The membership model includes generous return policies for most items and enhanced support for electronics, supporting member loyalty. — 10-K line 145: To promote member satisfaction, we generally accept returns of merchandise. On certain electronic items, we have a 90‑day return policy and provide, free of charge, technical support services, as well as an extended warranty.
- Beyond core warehouses, the company operates ancillary businesses—e‑commerce, business centers, travel services, and gas stations—that complement the warehouse model. — 10-K line 152: Other Businesses (includes e-commerce, business centers, travel, and other)
- Gasoline sales contribute roughly one‑tenth of total net sales, providing traffic to warehouses and a low‑margin ancillary revenue stream. — 10-K line 153: We operated 774 gas stations at the end of 2026. Our gasoline business represented 11% of total net sales in 2026.
- Digital channels account for about 11% of net sales, highlighting the importance of the online component of the membership model. — 10-K line 156: Our e‑commerce operations give members convenience and a broader selection of goods and services. ... Digitally‑enabled sales, which represents sales that are initiated through a digital device, ... represented 11% of total net sales in 2026.

## Market Positioning and Growth Outlook

- The firm acknowledges intense competition across multiple dimensions of retail performance. — 10-K line 191: Our industry is highly competitive, based on factors such as price, merchandise quality and selection, location, convenience, distribution strategy, and customer service.
- Key competitive set includes Walmart (Sam’s Club), Target, Kroger, Amazon, and other warehouse clubs such as BJ’s. — 10-K line 192: We compete on a worldwide basis with global, national, and regional wholesalers and retailers, including supermarkets, supercenters, online retailers, gasoline stations, hard discounters, department and specialty stores, and operators selling a single category or narrow range of merchandise. Walmart, Target, Kroger, and Amazon are among our significant general merchandise retail competitors in the U.S.
- The private‑label “Kirkland Signature” brand is positioned as a high‑quality, low‑price differentiator that contributes higher margins than national brands. — 10-K line 194: We believe that Kirkland Signature products are high quality, offered at prices that are generally lower than national brands, and help lower costs, differentiate our merchandise offerings, and generally earn higher margins.

## Valuation Insights

The price‑to‑earnings multiple derived from the calculator is elevated relative to typical market averages, implying a premium valuation that may reflect growth expectations.

## Future Developments and Strategic Recommendations

- New warehouse openings remain a primary net‑sales driver, though marginal gains may decline as site availability tightens. — 10-K line 405: We also achieve net sales growth by opening new warehouses. As our warehouse base grows and available and desirable sites become more difficult to secure, square footage growth becomes a comparatively less substantial component of growth.
- Membership growth, Executive‑member penetration, and renewal rates are identified as direct levers on profitability. — 10-K line 406: The membership format is integral to our business and profitability. ... The extent to which we achieve growth in our membership base, increase the penetration of Executive memberships, and sustain high renewal rates materially influences our profitability.
- Membership‑fee revenue grew 11% year‑over‑year, reflecting both higher pricing and successful acquisition/upgrades. — 10-K line 418: Membership fee revenue increased 11% to $5,907, driven by new member sign‑ups, membership fee increases, and upgrades to Executive membership;
- Overall net‑sales growth for FY 2026 was 10.1%, underscoring continued demand for the membership model. — EX-99.1 line 12: Net sales for the fiscal year increased 10.1 percent, to $297.2 billion, from $269.9 billion last year.
- Comparable sales grew 9.4% company‑wide; digitally‑enabled comparable sales grew roughly 20%, indicating a strong shift toward online purchasing. — EX-99.1 line 16: U.S. 10.7% ... Canada 5.0% ... Other International 7.0% ... Total Company 9.4% ... Digitally‑Enabled 19.5% ...
- A one‑time tariff‑refund benefit of $0.15 per diluted share boosted Q4 earnings, highlighting sensitivity to regulatory actions. — EX-99.1 line 22: Net income for the fiscal year was $9.226 billion... This year's fourth quarter was positively impacted by a non‑recurring benefit of $0.15 per diluted share from IEEPA tariff refunds received in the quarter, less partial reinvestment of those refunds in increased member values.
- The global footprint stands at 939 warehouses, with the U.S. comprising roughly 69% of locations. — EX-99.1 line 23: Costco currently operates 939 warehouses, including 647 in the United States and Puerto Rico, 115 in Canada, 43 in Mexico, 37 in Japan, 29 in the United Kingdom, 20 in Korea, 15 in Australia, 14 in Taiwan, seven in China, five in Spain, three in France, two in Sweden, and one each in Iceland, and New Zealand.
- Management identifies IT system reliability as a critical operational risk, implying ongoing technology investment is a catalyst for future performance. — 10-K line 244: Availability and performance of our IT systems are vital to our business. Failure to successfully execute IT projects and have IT systems available to our business would adversely impact our operations.
- Management states no material cybersecurity incidents have occurred to date, but acknowledges the inherent risk. — 10-K line 331: We are not aware of any risks from actual cybersecurity incidents that have materially affected us or are reasonably likely to materially affect us, including our business strategy, results of operations, or financial condition.

## Investment Recommendation

**Hold** — The firm’s membership‑driven warehouse model, anchored by a limited‑SKU approach and a high‑quality private‑label offering, continues to generate strong top‑line momentum and robust cash generation. Membership fee growth and accelerating digitally‑enabled sales underscore a resilient demand narrative, while a solid balance sheet with modest leverage supports ongoing dividend and share‑return capacity. However, the premium valuation reflected in the price‑to‑earnings multiple, combined with limited visibility on future warehouse roll‑outs, technology investment impact, and exposure to gasoline margin volatility, introduces uncertainty about upside potential relative to comparable peers.
- Risk: Intense competitive pressure from large general‑merchants and online platforms could compress margins.
- Risk: Diminishing availability of attractive warehouse locations may slow expansion‑driven sales growth.
- Risk: Execution risk around technology upgrades and digital initiatives, with no firm timeline disclosed.
- Risk: Sensitivity to fuel price fluctuations may affect ancillary gasoline margins.
- Risk: Macro‑economic headwinds could dampen discretionary spending and membership renewals.
